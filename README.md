# Jet Engine That Runs on Water

This repository documents a conceptual aerospace design for a hypersonic aircraft that combines atmospheric water harvesting, onboard power generation, electrolysis, and hydrogen-based propulsion.

This is a concept study and not a validated engineering design or flight-ready system.

## Overview

The proposed concept aims to address major challenges in sustained high-speed flight:

- long-range fuel requirements
- onboard power generation for aircraft systems
- heavy battery and fuel logistics
- efficient propulsion at hypersonic speeds

The idea combines several elements:

1. atmospheric moisture capture from humid air
2. onboard water storage
3. airflow-driven turbine power generation
4. hydrogen production by electrolysis
5. hydrogen-based propulsion in a scramjet-style flow path
6. water vapor as a major exhaust product

## Concept Summary

The aircraft is designed to collect water vapor from the atmosphere while flying through humid air or cloud layers. That water is stored onboard and then split into hydrogen and oxygen through electrolysis using electricity generated by a high-speed turbine mounted on the aircraft.

The hydrogen is then used as fuel in a high-speed combustion system to generate thrust. The concept is framed as a regenerative propulsion loop intended to reduce dependence on conventional fuel during extended flight.

## Core System Components

### 1. Atmospheric Water Harvesting

- wing-root or leading-edge intake scoops
- diffuser and condensation channels
- micro-fin or micro-channel cooling surfaces
- inertial separator and water reservoir

Purpose:
- capture atmospheric moisture
- condense it into usable liquid water
- provide a local water source for onboard fuel production

### 2. Aerodynamic Power Generation

- flush-mounted turbine on the fuselage or wing root
- curved airfoil-style blades
- high-speed rotor and integrated generator
- high-voltage DC output

Purpose:
- generate electrical power without relying on a large battery
- supply energy to the electrolyzer during flight

### 3. Electrolysis

- PEM or similar electrolysis stack
- water feed from the aircraft reservoir
- power input from the turbine generator
- hydrogen and oxygen output

Purpose:
- produce hydrogen fuel on demand
- convert harvested water into usable energy feedstock

### 4. High-Speed Combustion and Propulsion

- supersonic intake and compression section
- hydrogen fuel injectors
- combustion chamber or burn tunnel
- expansion nozzle for thrust generation

Purpose:
- provide propulsion for hypersonic flight
- reduce dependence on carbon-based fuels

## Mission Concept

The conceptual flight cycle is:

1. take off and climb using an initial onboard fuel reserve
2. enter high-speed atmospheric flight
3. capture water vapor from ambient air
4. generate electricity from the airflow turbine
5. split collected water into hydrogen and oxygen
6. use hydrogen in the propulsion system
7. continue the fuel-production cycle during cruise

## Why This Concept Is Interesting

This proposal explores a self-sustaining propulsion philosophy:

- reduced dependence on conventional aviation fuel
- use of atmospheric water as a resource
- onboard electricity generation from aerodynamic flow
- hydrogen as a high-energy fuel candidate

## Important Note

This repository is a concept exploration and not a validated aerospace engineering design. The performance figures and technical claims should be treated as theoretical assumptions rather than proven operational data.
